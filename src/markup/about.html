About PlateWatch

Food safety shouldn't be a guessing game.

PlateWatch brings regulatory information, restaurant evidence and community reports together so people can understand a restaurant's food-safety record before they eat.

Why we built it

One place to understand the record.

01Official data

What regulators have recorded. Licence status, suspensions, notices and reinstatements, each with a source and date.

02Restaurant evidence

What restaurants can demonstrate. Kitchen photos, certificates and logs, checked against published hygiene requirements.

03Community reports

What consumers observe. Moderated before publishing, and shown next to the restaurant's reply.

PlateWatch connects the three without pretending they are the same thing.

Trust principles

Built around evidence.

  1. 01

    Facts over verdicts

    We report what a regulator, restaurant or consumer has actually submitted. We never label a kitchen "dirty" or "safe".

  2. 02

    Source everything

    Important information carries a visible source and date, so you can check it yourself.

  3. 03

    Context matters

    An improvement notice, a suspension and a cleared status are not the same thing, so they never look the same.

  4. 04

    Show the journey

    A restaurant's record can change. We show that history rather than permanently label a business.

How PlateWatch thinks

Three streams in. One readable record out.

  1. Regulators publish official records
  2. Restaurants submit evidence
  3. Diners report what they saw
  4. PlateWatch labels, dates and verifies each stream
  5. You get one profile, with sources kept apart

FAQ

Questions people ask.

For owners, legal notices and data requests:

grievance@platewatch.in
Is PlateWatch an official government website?

No. PlateWatch is an independent information platform. Official regulatory information is clearly identified and attributed to its source.

Does PlateWatch certify restaurants?

PlateWatch can provide its own verification of submitted evidence, but this is distinct from official government or FSSAI certification and ratings.

How is restaurant information sourced?

From publicly available regulatory records, restaurant-submitted evidence and community reports. Each type of information is clearly identified.

Can restaurants respond to reports?

Yes. Restaurants can claim their profiles and respond to community reports or provide supporting evidence.

Can a restaurant's status change?

Yes. PlateWatch is designed around an evolving record. New regulatory information, evidence and verified updates can change what appears on a restaurant profile.

Can anyone submit a complaint?

Users can submit food-safety observations with supporting information. Reports are moderated and are never treated as verified regulatory findings.

Does a high PlateWatch compliance score mean a restaurant is officially certified?

No. A PlateWatch compliance score is distinct from official government certification or FSSAI ratings.

How does PlateWatch prevent false reports?

Reports are moderated, evidence can be requested, restaurants can respond, and official regulatory information is kept separate from community submissions.
